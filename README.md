## About The Forgotten Child

**The Forgotten Child** by **Anonymous** is a completed emotional family drama about favoritism, neglect, regret, revenge, and a daughter who finally stops waiting for her family to choose her.

In Wren's house, fairness is practically a law.

But it only applies to her twin siblings.

One share for Derek.

One share for Vanessa.

And somehow, never one for Wren.

Every gift must be divided between the twins. Every opportunity must be equal between them. Whenever either sibling feels overlooked, Wren is expected to sacrifice because she is the family's "easy one."

Her bedroom is taken away.

Her achievements are handed over.

Her needs are dismissed.

Even her twentieth birthday becomes another celebration for Derek and Vanessa.

For years, Wren waits for her parents to notice.

Then her grandmother gives her something no one else in the family ever has:

A birthday gift meant only for Wren.

It is an old silver heart necklace her grandmother has worn for forty years.

But even that is taken from her.

Vanessa claims the necklace.

Derek breaks it.

And when Wren finally demands that something belonging to her be returned, her own mother slaps her in front of everyone.

That is the moment Wren stops waiting.

She gathers the shattered pieces of her grandmother's necklace, packs her belongings, and leaves.

With a full-ride scholarship waiting in Canada, Wren finally chooses the life her family never imagined she could build without them.

By the time they understand what they lost, the forgotten child is no longer waiting to be remembered.

## Main Character

### Wren

Wren has spent her entire life being treated as the extra child.

Her twin siblings, Derek and Vanessa, constantly compete for their parents' attention.

Instead of teaching them to share, their parents repeatedly sacrifice Wren's needs to keep the twins happy.

Because Wren rarely causes trouble, her family assumes she needs less.

Because she survives every disappointment quietly, they assume she will survive the next one too.

But being strong does not mean being incapable of breaking.

When her grandmother's precious necklace is taken and destroyed, Wren finally understands that waiting for fairness will never make her family fair.

So she stops waiting.

---

## Derek and Vanessa

Derek and Vanessa are Wren's twin siblings.

They have grown up believing that everything must be divided equally between the two of them.

If Derek receives something, Vanessa expects the same.

If Vanessa gets an opportunity, Derek demands his share.

Their parents repeatedly give in to this competition.

But there is one person who is never included in their definition of fairness:

Wren.

---

## The Silver Heart Necklace

One of the most important objects in **The Forgotten Child** is the old silver heart necklace Wren receives from her grandmother.

It isn't valuable because of money.

It matters because it is one of the first gifts Wren receives that belongs completely to her.

For once, she doesn't have to divide it.

For once, someone remembers her.

When Vanessa takes the necklace and Derek destroys it, something inside Wren finally breaks with it.

She gathers every shattered piece.

And instead of continuing to beg for her place in the family, she leaves.

## From Forgotten Daughter to Independence

Wren spends years believing that if she remains patient enough, helpful enough, and understanding enough, her parents will eventually make room for her.

But every sacrifice teaches them the opposite lesson.

They begin believing Wren will always give way.

Her silence becomes permission.

Her maturity becomes an excuse to neglect her.

Her independence becomes proof that she doesn't need anyone.

Leaving changes everything.

For the first time, Wren's future isn't determined by Derek's demands, Vanessa's jealousy, or her parents' obsession with keeping the twins satisfied.

She can finally build something that belongs only to her.

---

## Toronto and a New Beginning

Wren's full-ride scholarship gives her something far more important than an education.

It gives her an exit.

Toronto represents a life outside the family dynamic that has defined her since childhood.

Her departure forces everyone she leaves behind to confront a reality they never expected:

Wren was not an unlimited resource.

She could leave.

And once she did, the empty space she left behind could not simply be divided between Derek and Vanessa.

## Why Read The Forgotten Child?

**The Forgotten Child** explores what happens when the person everyone takes for granted finally stops sacrificing herself.

This isn't simply a story about sibling rivalry.

It's about the damage caused when parents repeatedly reward demanding behavior while punishing the quiet child for being easier to manage.

Wren's journey is ultimately about understanding that she does not need her family's permission to have a life of her own.
